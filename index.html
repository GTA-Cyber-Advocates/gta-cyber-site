Why a community of practice

Security knowledge is everywhere. Reaching it is the hard part.

Cybersecurity expertise is already distributed across Global Tech Advocates. It sits with a CISO in one city network, a founder building detection tooling in another, a policy specialist somewhere else entirely.

What has been missing is the connective tissue. A new vertical chapter would have added another silo and asked those people to choose between it and the network they already belong to. GTA Cyber does the opposite. Members keep their existing GTA membership and their day jobs, and the community becomes the place where people practising the same craft meet, compare notes, and answer the call when any part of GTA needs a security perspective.

What we do

Four subjects we work on

Three at the front of what practitioners are being asked to solve, and one that nobody is being paid to solve, which is exactly why a volunteer network should.

01

Orchestrated digital governance

Discovery produces inventories. Governance requires an owner, a deadline and an audit trail for every asset, across security, GRC, legal and brand rather than in four disconnected tools.

02

AI and cybersecurity

Securing AI systems and using AI in security are two different problems, routinely discussed as one. Attackers adopted the technology faster because they face no procurement cycle.

03

Data sovereignty

Usually treated as a legal question. The security framing asks where data physically sits, which keys exist and who holds them, and what technically happens when a provider is compelled.

04

The grey digital divide

Our philanthropic strand. Services went digital by default; older people are most likely to be excluded and among the most targeted once online. Exclusion and victimisation reinforce each other.

Common questions

The short answers

What is GTA Cyber?

The cybersecurity community of practice within Global Tech Advocates. Not a regional chapter and not a vertical business line, but a cross-cutting group that draws on security practitioners already inside GTA networks and makes that expertise available across the whole organisation.

What is a community of practice?

A group of subject matter experts organised around a craft rather than a territory, a product or a business unit, sitting across the organisation rather than inside one part of it. Members keep their existing network membership and their day jobs, and the community is where people practising the same discipline meet, set standards and support everyone else.

Who can join?

Practitioners working in cybersecurity who are already part of a Global Tech Advocates network, and those who would like to join GTA in order to take part. Membership is free and volunteer-led.

How is it funded?

Through sponsorship, as the wider organisation is. Klarvant is the founding title sponsor. Sponsorship funds programme delivery, not salaries.
